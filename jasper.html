Jasper National Park · Alberta

Jasper is sold out. Overflow is a reservation, not a walk-up lot.

Parks Canada tells visitors that demand is heaviest from June through September and to have a camping reservation before coming to Jasper. Camping is permitted in designated areas only, with a valid camping permit and a national park entry pass. If Whistlers and Wapiti are full, the official in-park fallback is Overflow — which is reservable — or the first-come campgrounds listed for 2026. East of the park, William A. Switzer Provincial Park and private campgrounds near Hinton are the usual plan B.

Facts below were checked against official pages on 19 November 2026. Operating dates shown are as published for the 2026 season unless noted. Confirm 2027 dates, fees, and booking rules before you travel.

Designated sites only — no roadside camping

The official Jasper camping page states: camping is permitted in designated areas only with a valid national park camping permit and a national park entry pass. Do not camp or sleep in a vehicle at pullouts, trailheads, or day-use parking. If both reservable and first-come sites are full, leave the park for a provincial or private campground, or booked lodging in Jasper or Hinton. Do not invent a campsite on the shoulder of Highway 16 or the Icefields Parkway.

Reservable campgrounds inside the park

Four Jasper campgrounds were listed as reservable for the 2026 season: Whistlers, Wapiti (summer), Miette, and Overflow. Wabasso Campground and Whirlpool Group Campground “will remain closed for the 2027 camping season.” Wapiti was described as operating at reduced capacity for the 2026 summer season because of the 2024 Jasper wildfire. Parks Canada also said that in fall 2026 it would install perimeter fencing around Whistlers to reduce human-wildlife conflict after that fire — check current access notes before you arrive.

Campground 2026 operating season Sites Official notes
Whistlers 6 May–12 Oct 781, plus 17 oTENTiks in summer loops Largest campground in the park. Full service, electrical, and unserviced sites (15/30/50 amp on serviced sites). Showers included in the fee. Can take most motorhome and trailer sizes. Walk-in sites were listed as not available in 2025 — confirm current status. A trail through the campground is described as an easy walk or bike ride to town.
Wapiti (summer) 13 May–28 Oct 150 On the Athabasca River, minutes from the Jasper townsite. Electrical (15/30/50 amp) and unserviced. Showers included. Reduced 2026 capacity after the 2024 wildfire.
Miette 13 May–14 Sep 140 Near the east boundary of the park on the Miette Road. Unserviced and walk-in (no vehicles). Running water, fire rings, flush toilets, no showers. Best suited for tents and units under 8.2 m (27 ft).
Overflow 17 Jun–7 Sep 280 Reservable — not first-come. Primitive sites. Dry toilets, no showers, campfires are not permitted. Can accommodate most motorhome and trailer sizes. Official description: a mix of private, well-treed, and open sunny sites. The official camping page does not state the distance from town; confirm the location on a Parks Canada map before you drive.

Jasper camping fees are published by site type, not always by campground name. The official fees page listed, among others, primitive with no fire pit at $22.00, primitive with fire pit at $35.75, electrical with fire pit at $53.75, and full service with fire pit at $61.00 per night when this page was researched. Because Overflow does not allow campfires, check which official fee line applies. Entry passes and an $11.50 online reservation fee were also listed. Firewood is included in the cost of camping at Jasper campgrounds, according to the official camping page. Quiet hours, including a liquor, cannabis, and fire curfew, run from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m.

First-come campgrounds inside Jasper (2026)

The official 2026 summary listed these campgrounds as first-come, first-served, with no reservations possible. Several are along the Icefields Parkway south of town. Arrive early; they can fill, and they are not a guaranteed backup for a sold-out Saturday in July.

Campground 2026 operating season Sites Official notes
Snaring 13 May–28 Sep 62 Primitive with fire pit, plus walk-in sites with no vehicle access. Mix of private, treed, and sunny sites; some along the river. Best suited for tents and units under 8.2 m (27 ft).
Kerkeslin 13 May–14 Sep 42 Primitive, shady, along the Athabasca River. Official page: a short drive from Athabasca Falls and 34 km from the town of Jasper. Units under 8.2 m (27 ft).
Honeymoon Lake 27 May–14 Sep 34 Primitive, plus a bike camping area. Units under 8.2 m (27 ft).
Jonas 3 Jun–7 Sep 25 Primitive, with a bike camping area and walk-in sites. Campsite 14 is designated for bike camping only and can accommodate 10 tents. Units under 7.62 m (25 ft).
Wilcox 10 Jun–28 Sep 46 Primitive with fire pit, near the Wilcox Pass trail and the Columbia Icefield. Units under 8.2 m (27 ft).
Icefield (tents only) 10 Jun–12 Oct 31 Tenting only, including walk-in sites. Near the Columbia Icefield; official page notes glacier views from most sites.
Icefields Centre RV 13 May–12 Oct 100 Trailers and RVs only. Water and fire are not available on site. Official page: used for RV parking during the day and becomes an overnight option while exploring the Parkway.
Wapiti (winter) 28 Oct 2026 to May 2027 (closing date TBD) 40 First-come in winter; no reservations. Electrical (15/30/50 amp) and unserviced (fall and spring only, as listed).

Check 2027 booking status before you count on a walk-up

Parks Canada’s 2027 reservation calendar lists Jasper frontcountry camping — including Whistlers, Miette, Wapiti, Overflow, Snaring, Kerkeslin, Honeymoon Lake, Jonas, Icefield Centre tent, and Wilcox — as opening Tuesday, 26 January 2027 at 8 a.m. Alberta time, with a queue in place. Some of those names were first-come in 2026. Confirm on reservation.pc.gc.ca whether they remain walk-up or become reservable.

Provincial plan B: William A. Switzer Provincial Park

Alberta Parks locates Switzer Park 3 km west of Hinton on Highway 16 and 19 km north on Highway 40 (also listed as about 315 km from Edmonton and 510 km from Calgary). The park has five campgrounds along a chain of lakes: Gregg Lake, Jarvis Lake, Cache, Halfway, and Graveyard. Official text: Gregg Lake is the largest, with a mix of power, power/water, and unserviced sites, plus a booth, firewood sales, playground, showers, dumping station, and boat launch. Halfway and Graveyard are first-come, first-served campgrounds in a small meadow near the kettle lakes and Jarvis Creek.

The Gregg Lake campground page listed the season as year-round, with reservations 90 days in advance and bookings opening 13 February (confirm the current year’s opening date). A table visible when we checked showed 38 unserviced sites at $30 per night; the park overview still describes additional powered site types, so treat the full mix and rates as something to confirm on the live Alberta Parks page. Winter camping on Lakeside Loop (Loop L, sites 1–16) was listed as first-come. A drinking-water advisory posted 4 August 2025 and still noted on 5 November 2026 said hand pumps at Cache Lake, Jarvis Lake, and Kelleys Bathtub were unavailable — come prepared with your own water until the official advisory is lifted.

Approximate drive: Hinton is typically about 45–70 minutes east of the Jasper townsite on Highway 16, then you still have the drive north on Highway 40 to the campgrounds. Confirm on a current map. This is a real distance, not a “just outside the gate” backup, but it is a legal provincial alternative when the park is full.

How to use this on a sold-out weekend

  1. Check reservation.pc.gc.ca for Whistlers, Wapiti, Miette, and Overflow, including cancellations and availability notifications.
  2. Remember that Overflow is reservable and primitive: no showers, no campfires, and a short mid-summer season (17 June–7 September in 2026).
  3. If first-come sites are still offered in 2027, treat Snaring (closer to town than the Icefield campgrounds) or Kerkeslin (34 km south) as early-arrival options, not a late-evening sure thing.
  4. Book Gregg Lake or another Switzer campground, or a private site near Hinton, before you leave home.
  5. Do not camp outside a designated site inside the national park.
