Banff National Park · Alberta

Banff is sold out. Here is a legal plan B.

Most Banff frontcountry campgrounds are reservable and go quickly. For the 2026 season, Parks Canada listed only two first-come, first-served campgrounds, both on the Icefields Parkway, and said no overflow camping is permitted at Mosquito Creek. The practical backups are those walk-up sites if they are still offered, then Bow Valley Provincial Park and private campgrounds toward Canmore and Kananaskis — not a roadside pullout.

Facts below were checked against official pages on 19 November 2026. Operating dates shown are as published for the 2026 season unless noted. Confirm 2027 dates, fees, and booking rules before you travel.

No random or illegal camping in the park

Parks Canada’s Banff camping page states that camping is permitted in designated areas only, with a valid national park camping permit and a national park entry pass, and that camping is only permitted in a designated campground and site. The Banff park regulations page is explicit: camping, including sleeping in a vehicle, is not allowed in roadside pullouts, parking lots, trailheads, and day-use areas. The 2026 Banff visitor guide says it is illegal to sleep in your vehicle or RV on the roadside or at pull-outs, or to pitch a tent in an area that is not a campground.

Backcountry “random camping” in Banff is allowed only in designated areas, at least 5 km from a trailhead or designated campground, and only with a backcountry permit obtained in person or by phone from a Parks Canada visitor centre — it is not a same-day overflow substitute for a full frontcountry campground.

Reservable campgrounds inside the park

The official Banff camping page published this 2026 grid. Tunnel Mountain Village II and Lake Louise hard-sided stay open year-round. Almost everything else is seasonal and reservable. Parks Canada noted that Protection Mountain and Rampart Creek sites are reservation only, and that all Lake Louise campground sites are reservable. Soft-sided units at Lake Louise must stay in the soft-sided campground inside an electric fence because of seasonal bear activity; only hard-sided units are admitted to the hard-sided campground.

First-come campgrounds inside Banff (2026)

For 2026, Parks Canada listed only Mosquito Creek and Waterfowl Lakes as non-reservable, first-come, first-served. Both are on the Icefields Parkway, where Parks Canada says there is no cell phone coverage. Banff campground check-out is 11 a.m.; first-come campers often aim to arrive before then. Self-registration requires exact change or a credit card; debit is not accepted.

Check 2027 booking status before you count on a walk-up

Parks Canada’s 2027 reservation calendar lists Banff frontcountry camping — including Mosquito Creek, Waterfowl Lakes, Rampart Creek, and Silverhorn Creek — as opening Friday, 22 January 2027 at 8 a.m. Alberta time, with a queue in place. That listing may mean some 2026 walk-up campgrounds become reservable. Do not assume first-come status carries over. Check reservation.pc.gc.ca and the official Banff camping page close to launch.

Lake Louise overflow — confirm before you rely on it

The official Banff fees page lists a “Lake Louise – Overflow” rate of $13.50 per night. The main Banff camping page, however, does not include an overflow campground in the 2026 campground table and states that all Lake Louise campground sites are reservable. We have not found a current official description of how, or whether, Lake Louise overflow is operating. Ask at the Lake Louise campground kiosk or a Parks Canada visitor centre. Do not treat unofficial blog write-ups as the rule.

Provincial plan B: Bow Valley Provincial Park

These Alberta Parks campgrounds sit east of Banff, around Canmore, in Bow Valley Provincial Park (part of Kananaskis Country). A Kananaskis Conservation Pass is required to park a vehicle in Kananaskis and the Bow Valley. Alberta.ca listed personal-vehicle passes at $15 a day or $90 a year when this page was researched — confirm current rates and how many plates a yearly pass covers on the official page. Camping fees are extra. Reservations can be made 90 days in advance of arrival; the Alberta Parks booking window advances each morning at 9:00 a.m. Alberta time.

Private plan B near Canmore and Kananaskis

These are independently operated campgrounds, not Parks Canada sites. Check current season, site type, and rates on each operator’s site before you go. We are not listing prices unless the operator published a clear figure, and even then you should confirm at booking.

Private · Kananaskis Village

Mount Kidd RV Park

Official site: 229 serviced RV and campsites on the Kananaskis River at 1 Mount Kidd Drive, Kananaskis Village, open year-round. The operator says water and sani-dump are seasonal, while powered sites and showers stay open in winter. Booking window listed as up to 90 days ahead; maximum stay 14 consecutive nights. The homepage listed rates from $40.29 per night when checked — confirm the live rate for your dates. Drive time from Banff or Canmore depends on the route (Highway 1 then 40 / 742); often under an hour, but confirm on a map.

Nearby national park

Yoho National Park

If you can shift west into British Columbia, Yoho’s Kicking Horse and Monarch campgrounds are a legal Parks Canada alternative rather than an unofficial turnout. The official 2027 calendar opens those campgrounds on Tuesday, 19 January 2027 at 8 a.m. Mountain Time, with a queue in place. Book only through Parks Canada. Lake Louise village is the usual jumping-off point; confirm drive time on a map.

How to use this on a sold-out weekend

  1. Check reservation.pc.gc.ca for cancellations at Tunnel Mountain, Two Jack, Johnston Canyon, or Lake Louise, and set availability notifications if offered.
  2. If 2027 still has first-come sites at Mosquito Creek or Waterfowl Lakes, only drive up if you can arrive early and have a booked fallback. There is no overflow at Mosquito Creek.
  3. Book Bow River, Lac Des Arcs, Bow Valley, or Willow Rock on the Alberta Parks system, and buy a Kananaskis Conservation Pass.
  4. If provincial parks are also full, check Mount Kidd RV Park or other current private listings, then hotels or hostels in Banff or Canmore.
  5. Do not sleep in a pullout, trailhead, or day-use parking lot inside the national park.
