When the reservation grid is all red

The park is full. You still need a legal place to sleep.

Backup Loop is an independent plan-B guide for nights when Canadian national park campgrounds are sold out. We point you to first-come sites, official overflow where it exists, and nearby provincial or private campgrounds — never to illegal roadside camping.

Camp only in designated sites

In Canada’s national parks, camping is permitted in designated areas only, with a valid national park camping permit and a national park entry pass. Parks Canada’s Banff regulations state that camping — including sleeping in a vehicle — is not allowed in roadside pullouts, parking lots, trailheads, or day-use areas. “Random camping” in Banff’s backcountry is a separate, permit-only activity in designated areas, not a last-minute overflow option. If the park is full, go to a legal alternative listed here, or a hotel, hostel, or other booked lodging — do not camp illegally inside the park.

How to book

Win the launch, then keep a backup list

1

Create your account early

Parks Canada tells visitors to create a Reservation Service account before launch day. Book online at reservation.pc.gc.ca or by phone at 1-877-737-3783 (outside North America: 1-519-826-5391).

2

Know the 2027 launch mornings

Reservations for the 2027 visitor season open in January, and each location has its own date. Official calendar: Banff frontcountry camping on Friday, 22 January 2027 at 8 a.m. Alberta time; Jasper frontcountry on Tuesday, 26 January 2027 at 8 a.m. Alberta time. Both listings say a queue will be in place. Confirm on the Parks Canada reservations page.

3

Understand the queue

On busy launches, anyone on the waiting page 30 minutes before opening is randomly assigned a place in line at 8 a.m. local time. Arriving earlier in that waiting period does not improve your position. After 8 a.m. you go to the back. When your turn is called, you have 30 minutes to enter the reservation site. Official instructions: How to make reservations.

4

If your first site is gone

Parks Canada suggests using site and loop calendars, splitting a stay across sites, trying another loop or campground, and signing up for availability notifications for frontcountry camping. Then book a provincial or private plan B so you are not counting on a walk-up that may also be full.

5

First-come sites are not a sure thing

Where Parks Canada still offers first-come, first-served sites, plan to arrive before the 11 a.m. checkout common in Banff, have exact change or a credit card for self-registration (debit is not accepted at Banff self-registration kiosks), and have a backup if the loop is already full. Some campgrounds, including Mosquito Creek, do not permit overflow camping on site.

6

Provincial parks use a rolling window

Alberta Parks reservations use a rolling booking window that advances one day each morning at 9:00 a.m. Alberta time. Many Bow Valley campgrounds can be reserved 90 days before arrival. Book at albertaparks.ca/reservations or call 1-877-537-2757. A Kananaskis Conservation Pass is required to park a vehicle in Kananaskis and the Bow Valley.
